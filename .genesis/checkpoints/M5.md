## Quiz-me · Q+A · 2026-08-04

**Q1 — [Design]** Rejected job traces expire at 24h Redis TTL. Does that violate explainability
for compliance audits of PII-blocked candidates?

**A1:** For M5 alone, yes — there is a gap: rejected traces exist only in ephemeral job storage
(24h TTL) and are not yet durable. That is acceptable as a deliberate deferral to **M6**, which
ships the append-only `memory_audit` table and `GET /v1/memories/{id}/audit`. M6 will persist every
admit/reject (including PII-blocked) with actor, timestamp, and source turn ids before the job
expires. Admitted traces are already durable on the `memories.write_gate_decision` column.

**Q2 — [Edge case]** Should `GET /v1/jobs/{job_id}` disable HTTP caching to prevent cross-tenant
trace leakage via shared caches?

**A2:** Yes. Job responses carry tenant-scoped decision traces and must never be cached by
intermediaries. `read_job_status` now sets `Cache-Control: no-store`. Tenant isolation is enforced
in Redis keys and RQ meta; the cache header closes the CDN/browser side-channel.

**Q3 — [Change impact]** When M8 archives memories, what happens to write-gate traces on
rejected or archived candidates?

**A3:** Rejected candidates never reach `memories`, so M8 decay/archive does not touch them — their
only M5 artifact is the job trace (until M6 audit rows subsume that). For **admitted** rows, M8
sets `status = archived` and lowers `decay_weight`; it does not delete content or
`write_gate_decision`. Archive preserves the frozen admission trace for "why do you know this?"
Reflection summaries (also M8) are derived data linked to source memory ids, not replacements for
the original trace.
